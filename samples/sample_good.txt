Social media has changed how teenagers communicate, learn and see themselves, and the effects are mixed rather than purely good or bad. On one hand, platforms let young people keep friendships alive across distance, find communities around shared interests, and access educational content that was once out of reach. A teenager interested in coding, for example, can learn from tutorials and join open-source projects without leaving home.

On the other hand, constant exposure to curated images and public metrics such as likes can harm self-esteem. Studies have linked heavy use to sleep loss and anxiety, partly because the feeds are designed to keep users scrolling. Cyberbullying also follows teenagers home in a way traditional bullying could not.

The most reasonable conclusion is that the outcome depends on how social media is used. Limits on screen time, digital literacy lessons in schools and honest conversations with parents can preserve the benefits while reducing the harm. Rather than banning these platforms, society should teach teenagers to use them deliberately.
